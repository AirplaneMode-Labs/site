The promise

  • We pick the licence of each repository on day one and we will never relicense the core: that is Apache-2.0 for every browser library and the core runtime, and, for pagetriage, the MIT OR Apache-2.0 dual licence it already carried, as the table above states. The commitment covers pagetriage's dual licence.
  • There is no AGPL in the browser libraries.

What the commitment covers

Each point below is stated by a file in the repositories, cited in the page source.

  • The core is the six public repositories: pagetriage, glyphsift, localdata, runtime, dockit and site. They are under Apache-2.0, except that pagetriage is MIT OR Apache-2.0 and the site's prose and data are CC BY 4.0.
  • The open specs in runtime/spec are published under Apache-2.0 and never move behind the control-plane licence.
  • control-plane is private and is under FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0, which converts each version to Apache-2.0 on the second anniversary of the date that version is made available. No commitment beyond those terms is made.
  • Contributions are accepted under the licence of the repository they go to, as each repository's CONTRIBUTING file says: Apache-2.0 for glyphsift, localdata, runtime and dockit, MIT OR Apache-2.0 for pagetriage, and for the site Apache-2.0 for code and CC BY 4.0 for prose and data.

Why

Changing the licence of an established project causes forks and backlash: HashiCorp moved to BSL and OpenTofu forked within weeks; Redis changed licence and Valkey forked under the Linux Foundation. The lesson in our plan is to pick the licence on day one and never relicense the core. Permissive Apache-2.0 also carries a patent grant that enterprises look for, and it keeps embedding simple for developers who ship JavaScript and WebAssembly to their users.

What is paid

The paid layer is the control plane, which runs on the customer's premises. Per the plan it covers:

  • a signed private model and data registry with an offline sync and import channel;
  • policy: model allow-lists per device class, prompt-injection rulesets and data-retention controls;
  • audit logs and an evidence export (planned; no compliance claim);
  • SSO and SCIM for the admin console;
  • fleet capability and health telemetry, aggregated locally and exported on the customer's terms;
  • certified, reproducible builds with an SBOM;
  • Indic model packs;
  • support SLAs.

Pricing, terms and which of these ship first are not part of this page.
